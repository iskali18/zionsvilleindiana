Whether you’re looking for indoor activities for older kids and teens on a rainy day, during a cold Indiana winter, or when summer heat drives everyone indoors, this guide covers 32 activities near Zionsville and Northwest Indianapolis. Options for tweens and teenagers include climbing, karting, escape rooms, robotics, pottery, music, theater, sports, and other active, creative, and social experiences.

Ages are based on published guidelines when available. Many locations also offer activities for younger children, making them useful for families with siblings of different ages.

## Drop-In & One-Time Activities

These places are open for single visits, though some require timed tickets or advance reservations.

### Slick City Action Park

**Active & Indoor Slides · Recommended ages 9 through adult**  
**Location:** [4430 N. County Road 1000 East, Ste. 100, Brownsburg, IN 46112](https://maps.app.goo.gl/J5xy9GUrefjBhmrN9)

Slick City is an indoor dry-slide park with tall slides, steep drops, racing slides and launch-style attractions, plus areas for basketball and dodgeball. The main attractions are for ages 4 and up, with separate play areas for younger children.

Tickets are sold in 90- or 120-minute sessions, and guests under 18 need a waiver completed by a parent or legal guardian. Spectators do not pay to enter the park, though they still need a waiver. Slick City requires its own CitySocks, which are grip socks that must be purchased if you do not already have a pair. They can be reused on future visits if they are still in good condition. Grip socks from other parks are not accepted. Sensory Hours feature lowered music and smaller crowds for guests with sensory sensitivities.

[Visit Slick City →](https://slickcity.com/in-brownsburg/)

Hoosier Heights is an indoor climbing gym with options for beginners through experienced climbers. Visitors can try bouldering on shorter walls without ropes, climb taller walls using an automatic safety system that lowers them back down, or climb with a partner who manages the rope from the ground. First-time visitors complete an orientation before climbing, and kids 5 and under climb free.

Visitors can drop in during regular open hours without a reservation, and a day pass allows re-entry throughout the day. Climbing shoes are required and available to rent, while harnesses are provided at no additional cost for routes that use them. Everyone entering the facility needs a waiver on file; for anyone under 18, it must be completed by a parent or legal guardian.

### Laser Flash

**Active & Entertainment · Ages 7 through adult**  
**Location:** [617 3rd Ave. SW, Carmel, IN 46032](https://maps.app.goo.gl/r9bJHuxjF9TT4wE26)

Laser Flash has a large, two-level laser-tag arena and an arcade. A laser-tag session lasts about 40 minutes and includes three games, with up to 40 players able to play at once. Laser tag is open to players who are at least 7 years old or 44 inches tall.

Visitors can purchase single or multiple sessions, and reservations are recommended but not required.

### Fastimes Indoor Karting

**Active & Karting · Ages 8 through adult**  
**Location:** [3455 Harper Rd., Indianapolis, IN 46240](https://maps.app.goo.gl/RZDgkK5QQWW6bG6A8)

Fastimes has a two-level indoor go-kart track with racing options for kids, teens and adults. Junior karts are for drivers at least 8 years old and 4 feet 5 inches tall, and teen karts are for drivers at least 13 years old and 4 feet 9 inches tall. Younger children who are at least 44 inches tall can ride as a passenger in a two-seat kart with a parent.

Walk-in racing is available, but Fastimes recommends checking its website or calling ahead because events can affect track availability. Drivers under 18 need a waiver signed by a parent for each visit. Helmets and neck braces are provided, closed-toe shoes are required, and racers using a Fastimes helmet need a head sock, which is sold on-site.

**Accessibility & sensory note:** Fastimes uses gas-powered karts indoors, and some visitor reviews mention noticeable gasoline or exhaust fumes. Anyone who is sensitive to fumes should be aware of this before deciding to visit.

[Visit Fastimes Indoor Karting →](https://www.ftik.com/)

### IMAX Theater at the Indiana State Museum

**Entertainment & Film · Ages vary by movie**  
**Location:** [650 W. Washington St., Indianapolis, IN 46204](https://maps.app.goo.gl/stjmhHzaXgutbjBe9)

The IMAX Theater at the Indiana State Museum shows major theatrical releases along with documentary and educational films on a six-story, 84-foot-wide screen—the largest movie screen in Indiana. Many of the documentaries run about 45 minutes, and both 2D and 3D films are shown. Museum admission is not required, and an IMAX ticket includes free parking in the White River State Park garage and $2 off museum admission. Concessions include popcorn, candy, coffee and tea.

The oversized screen and powerful surround sound are noticeably different from a standard movie theater. In 3D screenings, special glasses add depth, so objects can appear to extend into the theater. Some films are also shown in IMAX 70mm, a large-format film process available at relatively few theaters.

**Accessibility & sensory note:** ADA-compliant elevators reach the theater, and the museum has accessible parking, power-door entrances and accessible restrooms. IMAX sound can include deep bass and sudden loud effects, so visitors who are sensitive to noise should keep this in mind before deciding to go.

### Indianapolis Motor Speedway Museum

**Racing History & STEM · Recommended ages 6 through adult**  
**Location:** [4750 W. 16th St., Indianapolis, IN 46222](https://maps.app.goo.gl/nHh2yBVLVjYGd2Zj6)

The Indianapolis Motor Speedway Museum reopened in 2025 after a major renovation, with galleries exploring the cars, drivers, engineering and history of the Indianapolis 500. The Qualifying Zone is the most interactive area, with racing simulators, a pit stop challenge, a strategy challenge and a dissected race car that shows how its components work together. The Starting Line Experience recreates the beginning of the race on a large LED screen.

The Innovation Workshop hosts scheduled K–12 STEAM programs, including field trips and camps, and track tours such as Kiss the Bricks run when track activity allows. Children under 5 are admitted free, Indiana Access Pass holders pay $5 in person, and Bank of America cardholders get free admission the first full weekend of each month through Museums on Us.

**Accessibility & sensory note:** Sensory bags with noise-reducing headphones, fidget toys and light-reducing glasses are available at the Welcome Center, along with a wellness room for a quieter break. The museum also has accessible parking, loaner wheelchairs, a wheelchair-accessible racing simulator and subtitles on the Starting Line video and tour narration. A licensed care provider receives a free admission ticket.

[Visit Indianapolis Motor Speedway Museum →](https://imsmuseum.org/)

## Libraries

Public libraries can be a good source of free indoor activities for tweens and teens, including gaming, Dungeons & Dragons, art and craft projects, makerspaces, clubs, workshops and volunteer opportunities. Several of the libraries below explicitly welcome nonresidents to teen programs or public spaces, while others set access requirements by program. We note those details in each entry so families can see what is available before visiting.

**Borrowing from other Indiana libraries:** Before purchasing a nonresident library card, check whether the library you want to use has a reciprocal borrowing agreement with your home library. If it does, eligible resident cardholders can borrow there without paying for a separate card. If not, Indiana’s [Public Library Access Card (PLAC)](https://www.in.gov/library/about/general-information/policies-of-the-indiana-state-library/plac-rules-and-guidelines/) provides borrowing privileges at public libraries across the state; the fee is $70 for one year as of October 2026.

**Location:** [675 S. Main St., Zionsville, IN 46077](https://maps.app.goo.gl/TLDeWyBHV6hdY5f47)

School of Rock makes band rehearsal and live performance a core part of its teaching model. Students in Rock 101 and the Performance program take a weekly private lesson along with a weekly group rehearsal, with the Performance program working toward live shows at local venues. Guitar, bass, drums, vocals and keyboard are all taught at the Zionsville school.

Programs progress by age: Little Wing is designed for ages 4–5, Rookies for ages 6–7, Rock 101 for ages 8–13 and Performance for ages 8–18; songwriting is also available beginning at age 12. The school also offers House Band and AllStars opportunities for more advanced students under 18.

[Visit School of Rock →](https://www.schoolofrock.com/locations/zionsville)

### Art & Pottery

#### SullivanMunce Cultural Center

**Art & Local History · Ages 6 through adult**  
**Location:** [225 W. Hawthorne St., Zionsville, IN 46077](https://maps.app.goo.gl/fSWWc5s4fEqTQZ9N7)

SullivanMunce Cultural Center brings together an art center, local-history museum and genealogy library downtown. Museum and gallery admission is free, so families can stop in to see rotating art exhibitions, local-history displays and artifacts without registering for a program.

#### The Wandering Peacock

**Pottery & Ceramics · Recommended ages 8 through adult**  
**Location:** [227 Jersey St., Westfield, IN 46074](https://maps.app.goo.gl/s2BJxERUNATi8HmSA)

The Wandering Peacock is a pottery studio where children, teens and adults work directly with clay rather than decorating pre-made ceramics. Classes include wheel throwing, where students shape clay as it spins on a pottery wheel, and hand-building, where pieces are formed by hand without a wheel. For tweens and teens, options include individual instruction, small-group classes and Spin with Friends, where four friends each work at a pottery wheel. Most wheel projects are fired in a kiln after class, and students come back later to glaze them for free.

Classes must be scheduled in advance; the studio does not offer regular drop-in pottery sessions. Options include beginner and more advanced instruction, hand-building workshops, parent-and-child classes, birthday parties and longer courses.

**Accessibility & sensory note:** The studio describes its atmosphere as colorful, inclusive and calming. Its Zen Den provides a private pottery-wheel room for one or two intermediate throwers who want more privacy while they throw.
